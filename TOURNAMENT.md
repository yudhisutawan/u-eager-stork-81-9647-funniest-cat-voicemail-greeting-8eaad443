# Tournament: Funniest Cat Voicemail Greeting

16 candidates: 8 local (CANDIDATES.md) + 8 Hermes (CANDIDATES-HERMES.md).
Every pair below was judged against the judge's published brief, criteria
in order: (1) cat voicemail + laugh, (2) cat-specific joke, (3) cat's own
voice/attitude, (4) spoken aloud, fast punch, (5) fresh/surprising.
Margins: 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelmingly better.

## Hermes candidates (for reference)

- H1 (109): Meow. Leave a message after the beep, unless you're opening tuna at 4 AM, in which case hang up and serve me.
- H2 (103): I'm busy pushing your coffee mug off the counter. Leave a message or don't, I'll ignore you either way.
- H3 (104): Your message means nothing. The Amazon box arrived and I own it now. Do not disturb the royal residence.
- H4 (106): You locked the bathroom door again? Utterly unacceptable. Leave a message before I shred the toilet paper.
- H5 (100): The red laser dot is a federal lie. Leave a message and I will continue staring blankly at the wall.
- H6 (94): I am currently sitting on your laptop deleting your unsaved work. Leave a message if you dare.
- H7 (74): If this is about the carrier box, the vet, or pants, lose my number. Meow.
- H8 (109): I am sleeping in a sunbeam. Your call is important to me, but nowhere near as important as this nap. Goodbye.

## Round 1 — local semifinals

- L1 vs L6: **L6 wins, margin 2.** Keyboard + emailed-the-boss is a fresher
  escalation than the laundry nap; L1's "pretend I didn't hear it" is good
  but L6's surprise is bigger.
- L2 vs L8: **L8 wins, margin 3.** "I ate the plant. It was delicious."
  deadpan + "preferably snacks" is much funnier than wall-staring, which
  also echoes H5's weaker half.
- L3 vs L4: **L4 wins, margin 1.** "The box is mine now... the box stays"
  is tighter and the punch arrives faster than L3's vet denial.
- L5 vs L7: **L5 wins, margin 1.** The 4 AM food demand is the judge
  brief's own exemplar of cat-specificity; "sounds like kibble" edges
  "I'll read it never".
- L6 vs L8: **L6 wins, margin 1.** Boss-email escalation beats the
  plant confession on surprise (criterion 5).
- L4 vs L5: **L5 wins, margin 2.** L5 carries two beats ("not a drill"
  + kibble) against L4's single box claim.
- L6 vs L5 (local final): **L5 wins, margin 1.** Criterion 2 favours L5
  (4 AM demand is the brief's canonical cat joke); criterion 5 favours L6.
  L5's voicemail structure is the more complete of the two.

Local champion: **L5**. Local runner-up: L6.

## Round 2 — cross-bracket: locals vs Hermes top

- L5 vs H1: **H1 wins, margin 2.** Same 4 AM food angle, but H1 is
  tighter: "hang up and serve me" is a perfect imperious cat command on
  the last words, while L5's kibble punch is wordier and softer.
- L6 vs H1: **H1 wins, margin 1.** H1's voicemail framing is cleaner and
  the demand more cat-specific; L6 is fresher but thinner as a greeting.
- L5 vs H2: **L5 wins, margin 1.** The mug is the most-told cat joke in
  the field (criterion 5 penalty); L5's kibble line is fresher.
- L6 vs H2: **L6 wins, margin 2.** Keyboard-boss is far fresher than mug.
- L5 vs H5: **H5 wins, margin 1.** "The red laser dot is a federal lie"
  is the most original phrasing in the field; it outweighs L5's edge on
  criterion 2.
- L6 vs H5: **H5 wins, margin 1.** The "federal lie" absurdity + blank
  wall stare beats the boss email on surprise.
- H1 vs H5: **H1 wins, margin 1.** The decider. H5 wins criterion 5
  clearly, but H1 wins criterion 1 (bigger laugh, reads more clearly as
  a voicemail greeting) and criterion 2 (the 4 AM demand is the brief's
  own example), and its punch ("serve me") lands harder than H5's
  trailing wall-stare.
- H1 vs H7: **H1 wins, margin 2.** H7 is short and punchy ("lose my
  number. Meow.") but H1 has more joke density and better greeting form.
- H1 vs H4: **H1 wins, margin 1.** Toilet-paper threat is funny; H1 is
  tighter.
- H1 vs H2: **H1 wins, margin 1.** Mug-knocking is the tiredest cat joke
  here.
- H1 vs H6: **H1 wins, margin 1.** Laptop-deletion menace is strong, but
  H1's demand + command is the better greeting.

## Winner

**H1 (Hermes candidate 1), 109 characters:**

> Meow. Leave a message after the beep, unless you're opening tuna at 4 AM, in which case hang up and serve me.

Why it wins: it is a complete voicemail greeting (criterion 1), built on
the brief's own exemplar cat behaviour — demanding food at 4 AM
(criterion 2) — in a clear imperious cat voice (criterion 3), with the
punch ("hang up and serve me") arriving on the last words (criterion 4),
in wording that is fresh rather than a worn cat pun (criterion 5). It
beat the strongest local (L5) by margin 2 and the freshest line in the
field (H5) by margin 1, and went 5–0 in the top-6 round robin — a clear
win over a strong baseline, so no rebuild was needed.

A tighter-punctuation variant was considered ("...after the beep — unless
you're opening tuna at 4 AM. Then hang up and serve me.", 107 chars) and
rejected: the original's "in which case" flow reads better spoken aloud.
